Transmission Links in Networks

Transmission links are an essential component of computer networks. They determine the physical medium used to transfer data between devices. Depending on the type of network and requirements, there are many specifications should considered

  • Bandwidth: The maximum data rate supported by the medium.
  • Distance: The length over which data needs to be transmitted without loss.
  • Cost: Budget constraints for installation and maintenance.
  • Reliability: The stability of the transmission over time.

The transmission links can be classified into two general types:-

Guided Transmission Links

Guided transmission links use physical media to transmit data, such as cables. These links are reliable and commonly used in local area networks (LANs). Below are some common types of guided links:

  1. Twisted Pair Cable: Used in telephony and Ethernet networks; inexpensive and widely available.
  2. Coaxial Cable: Used for cable TV and broadband; offers higher bandwidth compared to twisted pairs.
  3. Fiber Optic Cable: Uses light signals for data transfer; supports long distances and high speeds.

Unguided Transmission Links

Unguided transmission links use wireless media for data transmission. These are useful in wide area networks (WANs) and mobile networks. Examples include:

  1. Radio Waves: Used for Wi-Fi, cellular networks, and satellite communications.
  2. Microwaves: Employed in point-to-point links, often for long distances.
  3. Infrared: Short-range communication, such as remote controls and some wireless devices.
